Understanding your starting point

What it means to be a carrier

You are a carrier of a genetic variant (also called a mutation, a change in the DNA compared with most of the population) that can be associated with the development of a hereditary prion disease. This means there is an increased risk, not a certainty. Many people with this genetic condition live decades without developing any symptoms, and science is advancing rapidly toward preventive treatments.

Being a carrier is not the same as having the disease. It is a condition that gives you valuable information — and that information, used well, can become a real advantage.

  • Not all carriers develop symptoms. Penetrance —that is, the probability that the mutation will end up causing the disease— is high, although the age of onset varies considerably depending on the specific mutation and other individual factors that we still do not understand.

  • Knowledge is power. Knowing your genetic status lets you make informed decisions, plan for the future, and access specialized follow-up programs.

  • Science isn't standing still. There has never been more active research into prion diseases than there is right now. We are at a decisive moment for the development of preventive therapies.
